## Why it matters

Once you have the advantage, you want to keep it. A blockstring is a sequence of attacks made to be blocked: it keeps your turn going and pushes the opponent to make a mistake.

Pressure works on both sides of a match. Understanding it helps you attack with less risk, and it helps you survive when the opponent has their turn.

## How to do it

1. **Keep your turn.** Use a blockstring while the opponent is blocking, so they stay on defence.
2. **Leave small gaps.** Pressure works by leaving small gaps, called frame traps, that catch an opponent who presses a button.
3. **Mix in a grab.** When the opponent only blocks, grab them.
4. **End safe.** Finish every string with a move that is safe on block. Otherwise you hand over the turn and take damage.
5. **Defend patiently.** When the opponent has pressure, block patiently and look for the unsafe ending. Do not press buttons in small gaps.

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| Opponent blocks everything | Mix in a grab |
| Opponent presses buttons in your gaps | Keep using frame traps |
| You are defending against a string | Block, wait for the unsafe ending, then punish it |

## Common mistakes

- Ending a string with an unsafe move and getting punished.
- Never grabbing, so the opponent can block forever.
- Pressing a button in a small gap and walking into a frame trap.
- Using the same string every time, so the opponent learns where the gaps are.

## Practice

In training mode, build a short blockstring and check that its last move is safe on block. Then play a set and use it whenever the opponent blocks, adding a grab when they only block. On defence, set yourself a rule for the set: no buttons in small gaps; wait for the unsafe ending and punish it. After the set, note which of your strings got punished.
