## Why it matters

A combo is a sequence of hits the opponent cannot block once the first one lands. It is how one opening turns into real damage.

Long combos look impressive, but they are easy to drop under pressure. A dropped long combo does less damage than a finished short one, so reliability matters more than length.

## How to do it

1. **Start short and reliable.** Learn one short combo you can do every time before you try anything longer. A short combo you always finish beats a long one you sometimes drop.
2. **Practise on both sides.** Inputs are mirrored when you switch sides of the screen, so a combo that works facing right needs its directions reversed facing left. Train both until they feel the same.
3. **Learn to confirm.** Check that the first hit landed before committing to the rest. If it was blocked, stop and keep your guard up instead of finishing the combo into a block.
4. **Keep combos in perspective.** Combos only matter after you win the first hit, and the first hit is decided by neutral. The next lesson, "The Neutral Game", explains how to win it.

## Common mistakes

- Learning a long combo you can only finish some of the time.
- Practising on one side of the screen only.
- Finishing the whole combo even when the first hit was blocked.
- Spending all your practice on combos and none on neutral.

## Practice

Choose one simple combo and land it ten times in a row on each side of the screen. If you drop it, start the count again from the beginning, so the habit you build is finishing it. When that works, practise confirming: start the combo against an opponent who blocks some of the time, and only finish it when the first hit lands.
