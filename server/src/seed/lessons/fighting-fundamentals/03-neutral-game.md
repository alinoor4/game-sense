## Why it matters

Neutral is when neither player has the advantage and both are trying to land the first hit. Whoever wins it gets to attack, combo and apply pressure. Whoever loses it has to defend.

Combos and pressure only start after someone wins neutral, so it decides how often you get to use them. Getting better at neutral gives everything else you have learned more chances to matter.

## How to do it

1. **Control space with pokes.** Use your longest safe moves to keep the opponent at a distance that suits you.
2. **Play footsies.** Walk in and out of range, so the opponent attacks when you are just out of reach.
3. **Whiff punish.** When a move misses, hit the opponent while they recover from it.
4. **Use the triangle.** Attacks beat grabs, grabs beat blocking, and blocking beats attacks.
5. **Jump with care.** Jumping is high risk: a prepared opponent hits you out of the air with an anti-air.
6. **Read habits.** Watch what the opponent does repeatedly, then choose the option that beats it.

| Option | Beats | Loses to |
|--------|-------|----------|
| Attack | Grab | Block |
| Grab | Block | Attack |
| Block | Attack | Grab |

## Common mistakes

- Jumping in again and again against an opponent who is ready to anti-air.
- Pressing buttons at random when nothing is in range.
- Blocking every time, so a grab always works.
- Letting a missed move go unpunished.

## Practice

Play a set against a friend or online, and after each round name a habit the opponent showed, such as jumping whenever you walk backwards. Then choose the option that beats it, from the triangle or an anti-air, and use it the next time the habit appears. Between rounds, also remind yourself to hit any move that misses while the opponent recovers.
