## Why it matters

Your aim depends on how far the crosshair moves when your hand moves. If that distance changes from day to day, or from game to game, your hand never learns it and every flick turns into a guess.

A few numbers describe that distance. Once you understand them, you can choose a setting on purpose, compare it with other players and keep the same feel when you switch games.

## How to do it

1. **Know the two settings.** DPI is how many counts your mouse reports for every inch you move it. Sensitivity is the in-game multiplier applied to those counts.
2. **Compare with eDPI inside one game.** eDPI = DPI × sensitivity. It lets you compare settings within one game only, because each game scales sensitivity in its own way.
3. **Compare with cm/360 across games.** cm/360 is how many centimetres you move the mouse for a full turn. It is the measure that compares settings across games.
4. **Choose a distance that suits your game.** A lower sensitivity, which means more cm/360, gives your arm finer control. Most tactical-shooter players land between roughly 20 and 50 cm/360. Players of fast movement shooters often use less distance.
5. **Turn off mouse acceleration.** Acceleration moves the crosshair further when you move the mouse faster, so the same hand movement stops giving the same turn. In Windows, switch off "Enhance pointer precision".
6. **Pick one sensitivity and keep it.** Stay with it for at least two weeks. Changing it every day resets your muscle memory.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing eDPI between two different games.
- Copying someone else's sensitivity and changing it again after every bad match.
- Leaving mouse acceleration on, so fast flicks travel further than slow ones.
- Turning the sensitivity up to turn faster instead of using more of your arm.

## Practice

Write down your mouse DPI and your in-game sensitivity. Open the Sensitivity Converter in the Tools menu and read your eDPI and cm/360. If your cm/360 sits far outside the usual range for your type of game, choose one new value and commit to it. Then leave it alone for two weeks, however your next matches go.
