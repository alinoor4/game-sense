## Why it matters

Good crosshair placement gets you close to the head, but rarely exactly onto it. The last few pixels are a micro-adjustment, and they decide whether your first shot ends the fight or misses by a hair.

Because good placement turns most fights into micro-adjustments, you use this small movement in almost every duel. Making it reliable pays off in nearly every fight you take.

## How to do it

1. **Let placement get you close.** A micro-adjustment is a correction of a few pixels after your crosshair placement got you close. Keep your placement good so the correction stays small.
2. **Use your wrist and fingers.** Micro-adjustments come from the wrist and fingers, not the arm. Keep the arm still and let the small muscles do the fine work.
3. **Stop, then click.** Bring the crosshair to a stop before you click. Clicking while the mouse is still moving is the commonest cause of a near miss.
4. **Correct after a miss.** If your first shot misses, adjust and shoot again rather than spraying and hoping.
5. **Loosen your grip.** Tension ruins small movements. Hold the mouse only as firmly as you need to control it.

## Common mistakes

- Using the whole arm for a correction of a few pixels and overshooting.
- Clicking while the crosshair is still sliding towards the head.
- Spraying after a missed first shot instead of correcting it.
- Squeezing the mouse harder when the fight gets tense.
- Expecting micro-adjustments to rescue bad crosshair placement.

## Practice

Spend five minutes today on small targets that appear close to your crosshair. For every shot, correct with the wrist and fingers only, and make sure the crosshair has stopped before you click. If you notice your hand tightening, pause, loosen your grip and start again. Then spend another five minutes on the same kind of targets, and after any miss, adjust and shoot again instead of holding the trigger.
