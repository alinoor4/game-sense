## Why it matters

While you play, your attention is on the fight in front of you. Many mistakes happen outside that focus, so you never notice them in the moment.

VOD review means watching a recording of your own match to find the mistakes you cannot notice while playing. It turns a vague feeling of "I played badly" into a short list of things you can actually fix.

## How to do it

1. **Review losses, the next day.** Watch matches you lost, and watch them the next day, not while you are still annoyed.
2. **Pause at every death.** Ask three questions: what did I know, what did I do, and what would have been better?
3. **Sort each death.** Put it in one group: aim, movement, positioning or decision.
4. **Look for patterns.** Ignore one-off bad luck and look for mistakes that repeat. One repeated mistake fixed is worth more than twenty noted.
5. **Pick one focus.** Choose one thing to work on in your next session, and only that.

| Death type | A question to ask |
|------------|-------------------|
| Aim | Was my crosshair where the enemy appeared? |
| Movement | Was I still moving when I shot? |
| Positioning | Could more than one enemy see me? |
| Decision | Did I need to take this fight at all? |

## Common mistakes

- Reviewing only wins, where your mistakes are harder to see.
- Watching straight after the match while still frustrated.
- Writing down every small error and fixing none of them.
- Blaming every death on bad luck or teammates.
- Ending the review with several goals instead of one.

## Practice

Pick one lost match and watch the recording the day after you played it. Pause at every death, ask the three questions, and sort the death as aim, movement, positioning or decision. Write down the three mistakes that repeated most, then choose one of them as the only thing you work on in your next session.
