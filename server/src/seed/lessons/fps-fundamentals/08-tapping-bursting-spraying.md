## Why it matters

Holding the trigger feels powerful, but your accuracy drops while you keep firing and only recovers when you pause. How you fire has to fit the distance to your target.

Choosing the wrong method for the distance loses more fights than bad aim does. If you know when to tap, burst or spray, the aim you already have wins more duels.

## How to do it

1. **Tap at long range.** Fire single shots with a pause between them.
2. **Burst at medium range.** Fire 2 to 4 bullets, pause, and repeat.
3. **Spray up close.** Hold the trigger at close range, or when several enemies are close together.
4. **Respect the pause.** The pause lets your accuracy reset. If you shoot again too early, you waste the next bullets.
5. **Decide before you fire.** Judge how far away the target is, then pick the method.

| Method | Distance | What you must control |
|--------|----------|-----------------------|
| Tapping | Long | Waiting out the pause between single shots |
| Bursting | Medium | Stopping after 2 to 4 bullets, then pausing |
| Spraying | Close | The recoil while you hold the trigger |

## Common mistakes

- Spraying at a target far away, so most of the bullets miss.
- Bursting so fast that the pause never happens.
- Tapping slowly at an enemy who is right in front of you.
- Firing the same way in every fight out of habit.

## Practice

Stand in front of a wall at three distances: close, medium and far. At each distance, fire a spray, then several bursts of 2 to 4 bullets, then single taps, and compare the groups of bullet marks. Note which method leaves the tightest group at each distance. Keep going for ten minutes, and name the method out loud before you shoot until the choice becomes automatic.
