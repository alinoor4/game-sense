## Why it matters

Tilt is frustration that makes you play worse, which causes more frustration. Left alone, it turns one bad round into a bad evening.

Your aim and your decisions depend on a calm head. Catching tilt early protects the skill you have built, and it makes playing more enjoyable for you and your team.

## How to do it

1. **Know the signs.** You blame teammates, force fights, shoot earlier than usual or grip the mouse hard. Any of these means you are tilting.
2. **Reset between rounds.** Breathe out slowly, relax your hands, and name one thing to do next round.
3. **Set a stop rule before you start.** For example: two losses in a row means a 15-minute break. Decide it while you are calm, then follow it.
4. **Judge the decision, not the result.** A good play can still lose. Ask whether the choice was right, not whether it worked this time.
5. **Mute distractions.** Mute players who distract you. You do not owe anyone an argument.

## Common mistakes

- Queueing again straight after a frustrating loss to win it back.
- Arguing with a teammate in the middle of a round.
- Dropping a good habit because it did not work in one round.
- Making up the stop rule during the session, when you are already tilted.

## Practice

Before your next session, write your stop rule on a note next to your screen, for example: two losses in a row means a 15-minute break. During the session, do the reset between every round: breathe out slowly, relax your hands and name one thing you will do next round. When the rule triggers, take the break, even if you feel fine.
