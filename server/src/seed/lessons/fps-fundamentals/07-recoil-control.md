## Why it matters

When you hold the trigger on an automatic weapon, it kicks. Without control, only the first bullets go where you aimed; the rest climb over the target or drift beside it.

Recoil control keeps more of your bullets on target, so you can win fights that last longer than a few shots. Knowing how recoil works also tells you when not to spray at all.

## How to do it

1. **Learn the direction of the kick.** Automatic weapons kick first mostly upwards, then side to side.
2. **Move against it.** Controlling recoil means moving the mouse in the opposite direction of the kick: pull down first, then counter the sideways drift.
3. **Start with the first few bullets.** They are the most accurate and the most predictable. Learn them before you try to control the whole magazine.
4. **Learn your own weapons.** Every weapon has its own pattern. Learn the one or two you use most instead of every gun in the game.
5. **Let distance pick the method.** Spray at close range, burst at medium range and tap at long range.
6. **Practise against a wall.** The bullet marks show where your shots went, so you can compare them to where you aimed.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling straight down for the whole spray and ignoring the sideways drift.
- Trying to learn the full magazine before the first bullets are reliable.
- Practising a different weapon every session.
- Spraying at long range, where a tap would hit.

## Practice

Load a private game, stand facing a wall and spray one full magazine without moving the mouse. Look at the bullet marks: that shape is the kick you have to cancel. Spray again and pull against it, down first and then sideways, and compare the new marks with the old ones. Keep going for ten minutes with the weapon you use most.
