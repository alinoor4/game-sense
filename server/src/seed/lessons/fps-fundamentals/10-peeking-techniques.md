## Why it matters

Every time you step around a corner, you choose how much of yourself to show and for how long. A careless peek shows everything at once and gives the enemy an easy shot.

Different peeks solve different problems. Some gather information, some bait a shot and some take the fight. Choosing the right peek for the moment lets you decide when and how a fight starts.

## How to do it

1. **Peek with a purpose.** Before you move, decide what the peek is for: information, baiting a shot, or taking the fight.
2. **Jiggle peek for information.** Step out and straight back with `key:A` / `key:D`. You gather information or bait a shot without committing.
3. **Shoulder peek against snipers.** Show only your shoulder to make a sniper shoot. Then peek properly while they recover.
4. **Wide peek against tight angles.** Swing far out, fast. An enemy holding a tight angle has to move their crosshair a long way to reach you.
5. **Pre-aim and stop.** Aim at the spot before you peek, and stop with a counter-strafe before you shoot.
6. **Change it up.** Never re-peek the same angle the same way twice in a row.

| Peek | What it is for |
|------|----------------|
| Jiggle | Information or baiting a shot, without committing |
| Shoulder | Baiting a sniper's shot |
| Wide | Taking the fight against an enemy holding a tight angle |

## Common mistakes

- Peeking without knowing what you want from it.
- Jiggling so far out that you end up fully exposed.
- Shooting while still moving at the end of a wide peek.
- Repeating the exact same peek after the enemy has seen it.

## Practice

Find a corner in a private game and jiggle-peek it 20 times: step out with `key:A` or `key:D` and straight back, and check that you never over-expose yourself. Then practise wide peeks on the same corner: pre-aim the spot, swing out fast, counter-strafe and fire. Five minutes is enough if you stay focused on how much of you each peek shows.
