## Why it matters

Many duels are decided before anyone aims. Two things decide who sees the other first: where each player stands relative to the corner, and the delay of the network.

If you understand both, you can choose positions that let you see first, and avoid the ones where the enemy always seems to get the jump on you.

## How to do it

1. **Stand away from the corner.** Around a corner, the player standing farther from it sees the opponent's shoulder before their own eyes are exposed. Whoever hugs the corner is seen first.
2. **Hold from distance.** When you hold an angle, stand back from the corner. When you peek, do not hug the wall you are peeking around.
3. **Know peeker's advantage.** This is a network effect. The peeker's movement reaches the defender's screen slightly late, so the peeker sees the defender first by a fraction of a second.
4. **Respect the combination.** The two effects combine. A peeker who is far from the corner and moving fast is very hard to hold.
5. **Counter it as a defender.** Play an off-angle, an unexpected position the peeker does not check first. Hold from farther back. Or set up a crossfire with a teammate.

| You are | What works for you |
|---------|--------------------|
| Holding an angle | Distance from the corner, an off-angle or a crossfire |
| Peeking | Distance from the wall you peek around, and speed |

## Common mistakes

- Holding an angle right next to the wall, where a peeker sees you first.
- Peeking with your shoulder against the wall you are peeking around.
- Holding the same obvious spot every round against a fast peeker.
- Blaming the network for every lost duel instead of checking your position.

## Practice

In your next match, notice where you stand each time you hold an angle, and take a step back from the corner whenever the space allows it.
