## Why it matters

Some games let you move faster than normal running allows. Bunny hopping and slide cancelling keep or build speed, which helps you reposition quickly and makes you a harder target.

These are game-specific techniques. They do not exist in every game, and updates often change them. They also win far fewer rounds than crosshair placement, so treat them as an extra on top of solid fundamentals, not a replacement for them.

## How to do it

1. **Check your game before you invest.** Make sure the technique currently works in your game before you spend hours on it. Test it yourself in a private game.
2. **Bunny hop by jumping as you land.** Jump again at the moment you land, and turn in the air by air-strafing: hold `key:A` or `key:D` and move the mouse the same way. Friction never gets a chance to slow you.
3. **Slide cancel to keep momentum.** In some movement shooters, you start a slide and interrupt it early. You keep your momentum and recover faster.
4. **Use it to reposition.** Movement tech is for getting to a new position and for being a harder target, not for shooting while flying through the air.
5. **Remember the accuracy rules.** They still apply. Slow down or stop before you shoot, as you would without the technique.
6. **Learn it last.** Only practise movement tech after your fundamentals are solid.

## Common mistakes

- Spending hours on a technique that no longer works in your game.
- Shooting in the middle of a hop and expecting the bullets to land.
- Learning movement tech before crosshair placement.
- Using the extra speed to run into fights instead of to reposition.

## Practice

Start by confirming in a private game that bunny hopping works in the game you play. Then try to chain ten jumps without losing speed: jump again the moment you land, and air-strafe with `key:A` or `key:D` while moving the mouse the same way. Count how many jumps you chain before you slow down, and spend ten minutes trying to reach ten.
