## Why it matters

Your team can only act on what they hear and understand. Long, emotional or vague calls waste the short moment in which information is useful, and they bury the details that matter.

Clean communication turns what you see into decisions your teammates can make. It also keeps the team calm, which helps everyone play better.

## How to do it

1. **Keep calls short and complete.** Say how many, where, and what they are doing. For example: "Two, A Main, pushing."
2. **Use callouts.** Name places with the map's callouts, not "over there" or "behind the thing".
3. **Give information, not emotion.** Report what happened, without blame or anger.
4. **Announce your plans.** Say what you are going to do before you do it, such as "flashing A in three".
5. **Respect the last player alive.** When a teammate is the last one alive, stay quiet unless they ask. They need to hear the game.
6. **Keep the debrief short.** After the round, one sentence about the next round is enough.

## Common mistakes

- Describing your own death in detail while teammates are still fighting.
- Saying "he's there" without saying where "there" is.
- Blaming a teammate over voice chat.
- Talking over the last player alive.

## Practice

Play one match in which every call you make follows the same format: how many, where, what they are doing. Before you throw utility or push, say what you are about to do. When a teammate is the last one alive, say nothing unless they ask. After the match, think of a call you made that was too long and say how you would shorten it.
