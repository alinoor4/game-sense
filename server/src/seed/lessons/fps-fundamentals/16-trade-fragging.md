## Why it matters

A trade is killing the enemy who just killed your teammate, while that enemy is still exposed and focused on the fight they just won. It turns a lost duel into an even exchange.

Teams that trade well rarely lose a player for nothing. A one-for-one trade usually favours the attacking side, because it opens space on the map that the defenders now have to win back.

## How to do it

1. **Stay close.** To be tradable, the first player must be followed closely, about one to two seconds behind.
2. **Watch your teammate's fight.** As the second player, watch the first player's fight, not a different angle.
3. **Punish the killer.** The moment your teammate dies, the enemy who killed them is still exposed and focused. Take that fight straight away.
4. **Do not dry peek.** When a teammate is two seconds away, do not go in alone. Wait and go together.
5. **Know your job as the entry.** When you go in first, your job is to create space and information. Dying while your teammate trades you is a success.

## Common mistakes

- Following so far behind that the enemy has time to reset after the first kill.
- Watching a different angle instead of your teammate's fight.
- Dry peeking alone when a teammate is seconds away.
- Treating your death as the entry as a failure, even when it was traded.

## Practice

Play one match in which, on every push, you agree who goes first and who follows. As the second player, stay about one to two seconds behind and keep your crosshair on the first player's fight. After the match, count how many of your team's deaths were traded, and for each death that was not, note how far away the nearest teammate was.
