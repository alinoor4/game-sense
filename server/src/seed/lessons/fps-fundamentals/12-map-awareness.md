## Why it matters

Many surprises in a match are not really surprises: the information was there, but you did not collect it. Map awareness means always knowing where enemies could be, where they have been seen and where your teammates are.

With that picture in your head, you take fights on your terms, avoid walking into enemies you could have expected, and make better decisions about where to go next.

## How to do it

1. **Learn the callouts.** Callouts are the shared names of places on a map. Information is only useful to others if everyone knows which place you mean.
2. **Check the minimap often.** Glance at it every few seconds, and always after a kill or a death appears in the feed.
3. **Listen.** Sound is information. Footsteps, reloads and ability sounds tell you where someone is without you seeing them.
4. **Count the enemies.** If four enemies are seen on one side, the other side has at most one. Use what you know to work out what you do not.
5. **Track your teammates.** Know where your own team is, so you know which areas are covered and which are empty.

## Common mistakes

- Watching only your crosshair and never the minimap.
- Playing with music or voice chat so loud that you miss footsteps.
- Using your own names for places instead of the shared callouts.
- Forgetting an enemy you saw a few seconds ago because they moved out of view.

## Practice

Play one match in which you call every enemy position you learn out loud, using the proper callouts, even if nobody is listening. Every time a kill or a death appears in the feed, glance at the minimap and say where the remaining enemies probably are. Before you play, you can practise reading callouts in the Lineup Maps tool in the Tools menu.
