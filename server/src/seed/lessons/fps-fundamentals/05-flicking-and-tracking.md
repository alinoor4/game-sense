## Why it matters

Some targets appear suddenly and you have to snap to them. Others keep moving and you have to stay on them. Snapping is flicking, staying on is tracking, and they need different kinds of movement.

Knowing which skill a fight asks for tells you what to practise. Training the skill your game rewards gives you more improvement for the same time.

## How to do it

1. **Flick to sudden targets.** A flick is a fast move from where your crosshair is to the target, then a click. Use it against targets that appear suddenly.
2. **Break the flick into three parts.** Fast move, small correction, click. Clicking before the correction is the usual miss.
3. **Track moving targets.** Tracking means keeping the crosshair on a moving target continuously. You need it with automatic fire and in movement-heavy games.
4. **Match the speed smoothly.** When tracking, move at the target's speed instead of chasing it in jerks. Stay relaxed.
5. **Accuracy first, speed second.** Slow down until you hit about 90% of your shots, then speed up.
6. **Train what your game rewards.** Tactical shooters reward flicking and crosshair placement. Arena and battle-royale shooters reward tracking more.

## Common mistakes

- Clicking during the fast part of a flick, before the correction.
- Chasing a moving target in jerks instead of matching its speed.
- Going faster while most of your shots still miss.
- Training only the skill you already enjoy instead of the skill your game rewards.

## Practice

Spend five minutes on each skill today. Start with flicks at a pace where you land about 90% of your shots, and say the three parts to yourself as you go: move, correct, click. Then switch to tracking and focus only on smooth, relaxed movement at the target's speed. Do both in the flick and tracking modes of the demo below.
