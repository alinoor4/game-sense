## Why it matters

Where you stand decides how many enemies can shoot you and how easily your team can help. A strong aimer in a bad position often loses to a weaker aimer in a good one.

Good positioning makes everything else easier. With the right spot, you need fewer perfect shots to win a fight, and a mistake is less likely to cost you the round.

## How to do it

1. **Check the three things.** A good position gives you cover, an escape route and a teammate who can help. If a spot has none of the three, leave it.
2. **Isolate angles.** Fight one enemy at a time. Use walls so that only one opponent can see you.
3. **Move after you are seen.** Change position after a kill or after being seen. The enemy now knows where you are.
4. **Fit the spot to your weapon.** Height and distance should suit the gun you carry.
5. **Set up crossfires.** In a crossfire, two teammates watch the same area from different angles, so the enemy cannot face both.
6. **Rotate from cover to cover.** Do not stand in the open while you rotate to another part of the map.

## Common mistakes

- Holding a spot with no cover and no way out.
- Standing where several angles can see you at once.
- Staying in the same spot after a kill.
- Taking a long-range position with a close-range weapon.
- Crossing open ground during a rotation instead of moving between cover.

## Practice

Play one match and, each time you stop to hold a position, check it against the three things: cover, an escape route and a teammate who can help. If a spot has none of them, move. After every kill, and every time you are seen, change position before the enemy can react. After the match, think back over your deaths and note how many came from staying too long in one place.
