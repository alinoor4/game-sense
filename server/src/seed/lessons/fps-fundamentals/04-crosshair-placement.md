## Why it matters

Most duels are decided before anyone shoots. If your crosshair is already at head height on the spot where an enemy will appear, you only have to click. If it is pointing at the floor, you have to move a long way under pressure, and long movements miss.

Good crosshair placement makes average aim look great. It is the cheapest improvement you can make.

## How to do it

1. **Head height, always.** Find head level on a wall or door frame and keep the crosshair on that line while you move. It should never rest on the floor or the sky.
2. **Aim where they will be, not where the wall is.** Leave a small gap between the corner and your crosshair, about one body width. An enemy who walks out lands on your crosshair instead of behind it.
3. **Clear one angle at a time.** As you round a corner, move your crosshair to the next place an enemy could be before that place comes into view.
4. **Let movement do the aiming.** When you strafe out with `key:A` or `key:D`, your crosshair sweeps across the angle for you. Keep the mouse still and let the enemy walk into it.
5. **Adjust for distance.** A far enemy looks lower on your screen than a near one. Re-check head level every time the distance changes, especially on stairs and slopes.

## Common mistakes

- Looking at the ground while running between fights.
- Hugging the corner with the crosshair, so you have to flick after the enemy appears.
- Aiming at chest height "to be safe". A chest-level crosshair needs a correction for every headshot.
- Forgetting height changes: ramps, boxes and stairs all move head level.
