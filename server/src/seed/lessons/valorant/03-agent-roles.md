## Why it matters

Every agent belongs to a role, and each role has a job. When players ignore their role, a team can end up with everyone doing the same thing and nobody covering the rest.

Knowing your role tells you what to do and where to be in every round. It also tells you what to ask from your teammates, and what they can expect from you.

## How to do it

1. **Learn the four roles.** Duelists create space and take first fights. Initiators gather information and set up pushes. Controllers block vision with smokes. Sentinels lock down areas and watch flanks.
2. **Balance the team.** A balanced team usually has smokes and at least one source of information.
3. **Master one or two agents.** Play them until their abilities are automatic. Then your attention is free for the round instead of for your buttons.
4. **Play where your role belongs.** Your role tells you where to be. A Duelist at the back of a push is not doing the job.

| Role | Job | Asks from teammates |
|------|-----|---------------------|
| Duelist | Create space and take first fights | Follow closely, so a lost fight can be traded |
| Initiator | Gather information and set up pushes | Move as soon as the information or setup lands |
| Controller | Block vision with smokes | Say which sightlines need blocking |
| Sentinel | Lock down areas and watch flanks | Leave the flank to them and focus forward |

## Common mistakes

- Picking an agent for its abilities and ignoring the role's job.
- Changing agents every match, so the abilities never become automatic.
- Starting a round with no smokes and no source of information on the team.
- A Duelist waiting behind the rest of the team.

## Practice

Choose one role and play one or two agents from it in your next few sessions. Before every round, say your job in a sentence, such as "I take the first fight" or "I watch the flank". After each match, check the table above and ask whether you did your role's job, and where you were standing when you died.
