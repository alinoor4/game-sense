## Why it matters

A lurker plays away from the team to catch rotating defenders or attack from behind. Done well, it splits the enemy's attention and wins fights nobody saw coming.

Done badly, it costs the team a player. A lurk that gets no kill and no information means your team fought four against five for nothing.

## How to do it

1. **Time your arrival.** Timing is everything. Arrive when the enemy's attention is on your team, not before.
2. **Listen and report.** Listen for rotations and tell your team what you hear, even when you get no kill.
3. **Make it count.** Leave the lurk with a kill or with information. With neither, your team simply played a player down.
4. **Keep it rare.** Do not lurk every round. A lurk works because it is unexpected.
5. **Expect the flank on defence.** As a defender, assume someone will come from behind. A teammate, or a sentinel ability, should watch it.

| Lurk result | What it gave the team |
|-------------|-----------------------|
| A kill on a rotating defender | An easier fight for everyone else |
| Information about rotations | Calls the team can act on |
| No kill and no information | Four against five for nothing |

## Common mistakes

- Arriving before the enemy is busy with your team, and fighting alone.
- Lurking in silence and never reporting what you hear.
- Lurking every round until the enemy expects it.
- Defending with nobody, and no ability, watching behind.

## Practice

In your next match, choose in advance the few rounds in which you will lurk. In each of them, wait until you hear your team engage before you move, and call every rotation you hear. On defence, agree before the round who, or which ability, watches the flank. After the match, count your lurks and note how many brought a kill or information.
