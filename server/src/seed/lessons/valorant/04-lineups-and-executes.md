## Why it matters

Throwing abilities by feel works some of the time. A lineup makes it work every time: a fixed standing spot and aim point send an ability to the same place, even under pressure.

An execute is the team version. Instead of everyone using abilities whenever they like, the team takes a site with a plan, and each piece of utility does its job in the right order.

## How to do it

1. **Know what a lineup is.** It is a fixed standing spot and aim point that sends an ability to the same place every time.
2. **Start with one that matters.** Learn one useful lineup, for example a post-plant lineup, before collecting dozens.
3. **Plan the execute.** An execute is a planned site take. Smokes block the defenders' sightlines, flashes or information abilities go in, then the team enters together.
4. **Keep the order.** Vision blockers first, then blinds, then people.
5. **Record each lineup.** Note where you stand, where it lands and the steps to throw it.

| Order | What goes in | Why |
|-------|--------------|-----|
| First | Vision blockers | Defenders cannot see the entry |
| Then | Blinds and information | Defenders are blinded or found |
| Last | People | The team enters together |

## Common mistakes

- Collecting lineups you never use in a match.
- Entering the site before the smokes are down.
- Throwing blinds after the team has already entered.
- Standing slightly off the spot and expecting the same result.

## Practice

Pick one lineup for your main map, ideally one you can use after the plant. Practise it in a private game until it lands five times in a row. To see how a lineup is recorded, with where you stand, where it lands and the steps, look at the Lineup Maps tool in the Tools menu.
